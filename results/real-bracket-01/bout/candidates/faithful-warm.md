# Riverbend Reading Corps: a promising first year, with the real test still ahead

The Riverbend Reading Corps spent 2025-26 doing one thing: sitting down with young readers. By the end of the year, tutors had worked with 4,218 students in grades 1 through 3 at 37 schools, in 30-minute sessions held three times a week.

The early numbers are encouraging. Tutored students gained an average of 11 scale-score points on the district's spring reading screener, against 8 points for students who were not tutored. But this was an internal pilot comparison and it was not randomized: the students chosen for tutoring may have differed from the others in ways that explain some or all of the gap.

The year cost $2.9 million, approximately $688 for each student served.

The program's evaluator, Dr. Lena Okafor, is careful on this point. "The results are encouraging but do not yet show that tutoring caused the gains," she wrote.

The question of cause goes to a randomized study planned for 2026-27. Until then, these results are best described as preliminary.
